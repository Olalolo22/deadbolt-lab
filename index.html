DeadBolt stitches three technologies that were never meant to touch: 1979 cryptography, a Web2 heartbeat and an on-chain clock. Together they guard what only you know (keys, passwords, the letter you never sent) while you live, and hand it to your family when you don't. No lawyer reads it, and no server decides you're gone.

The problem

The anatomy

One creature, three stolen parts.

Mary Shelley stitched a body in 1818. We stitched a protocol. Each part is useless alone; sewn together, they do something none of them can do on their own.

Part I · 1979

The Brain

Shamir's Secret Sharing

Your vault key is split into three shards. Any two rebuild it, and one alone is mathematically meaningless noise. It's pure algebra from 1979, with no blockchain needed.

Part II · Web2

The Heart

Email & Telegram heartbeat

Every 30 days, a one-tap "I'm alive" link lands in your inbox or Telegram. No wallet or gas needed, so your mum could do it. Miss three in a row, and the alarms start.

Part III · On-chain

The Hand

Time-locked release on a chain clock

The third shard sits behind a chain clock and opens only after the heartbeat and the grace period have both run out. No server and no admin key can open it early, not even ours.

The experiment

Four states. One direction.

  1. 01

    Alive

    You tap the pulse link and the timer resets. Nothing moves.

  2. 02

    Flatline

    You miss a check-in. DeadBolt reminds you, up to three times. Nothing else happens.

  3. 03

    Grace

    Three missed in a row opens 14 days of alarms on every channel. One tap undoes everything.

  4. 04

    Resurrection

    The clock releases shard C to your heir's account, and only theirs. They add their card and the secret lives again.

Seal your backup

Pick a password only you know. Without it this file is useless, even to your heir. There's no reset, so write the password down somewhere away from the file.

What is this backup for?

Two things. While you're alive, it's your spare piece. And it's a break-glass route: keep the file in your safe and the password in your will, or with your lawyer. If the chain or DeadBolt ever goes silent, your heir's card plus this backup plus the password still opens the vault.

Only leave it with someone you'd trust with the vault itself. Together with your heir, they could open it early.
